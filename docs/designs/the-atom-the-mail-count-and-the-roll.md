## Problem Statement

Joseki has four places where people talk and no place where people are.

Table talk lives in a game and dies with the route. Club halls live behind a club
you have to join and a screen you have to visit. The Post — one permanent thread
per pair, already written, already correct — is buried inside the Profile screen,
and nothing anywhere in the app says "write to them." The trainer's letters are the
only thing in the product that has ever shown an unread count, and that trainer is
a bot.

The result is an app that feels empty when nobody is playing. A returning player
lands on a dashboard of their own progress and learns nothing about whether anyone
else was here.

The ask was a mail icon in the top bar, a collapsible chat sidebar, and "almost a
social media for go too." Those are not three features. They are one missing
primitive and three containers for it.

## What Makes This Cool

**The unit of content is a position, and the reply is a move.**

Every social app has a post with a picture attached. Joseki can have a post that
is *a board*, and a reply that is *a move on that board* — legality-checked by the
engine, named by `relations.js` (which already knows twelve shapes), and comparable
against what the shipped KataGo human net would have played. The reply is
verifiable. No general-purpose social app can ever have that, because none of them
own an engine that understands the answer.

Nearly every part already exists and is wired to nothing:

- `src/engine/sgf.js` serialises and parses records.
- `src/components/Board.jsx` + `boardGeometry.js` + `stoneArt.jsx` render at any size.
- `src/views/tableTalk.js` already reads "the cut at D4" out of prose and lights D4.
- `server/room.js` already renders review frames of one position to several people.
- `src/engine/review.js` already refuses an illegal line on the server exactly as the board does.

What is missing is a **diagram atom**: a whole board, plus a crop to look at it
through. Ship that once and a letter can carry a position, a roll row can *be* a
position, and a hall message can hold one.

**The second thing worth naming:** "almost" was the honest word in the brief. The
problem being solved is arriving in an empty room. The fix is evidence that the
club was here — not a place to broadcast. If nobody in the Go Guatemala association
ever composes an original post and the roll is still good, the right thing was built.

## Constraints

From `CLAUDE.md`, `.claude/rules/`, and the privacy document:

- **Rules never live in a view.** The atom's legality check belongs in
  `src/engine/`, not in the component that draws it.
- **The engine is pure.** No React in `src/engine/`. It has to be able to move to a
  server — and in this design it does: the Worker validates a replied move with the
  same code the board uses.
- **Two-shadow neumorphism, Lucide icons only.** The unread dot, the dock and the
  roll card are raised or sunken by the same two shadows as everything else.
- **Type scale floor is 12px.** An unread count carries meaning, so it cannot be 10px.
- **The stylesheet names no colour and no font family, only tokens.** A tint composed
  from another token resolves in `tokensFor`, never in `css.js`.
- **`src/styles/css.js` is one template literal.** A backtick anywhere in a comment
  ends the string and breaks the build.
- **Nine languages, Hebrew right-to-left.** Every new string needs a catalogue line
  in all nine; the parity suite refuses a missing line, an invented key, or an
  overlay naming something the data lacks. A right-hand dock mirrors in RTL and the
  board inside it does not.
- **`src/content/legal.js` is a contract, not boilerplate.** It enumerates every key
  Joseki stores, on the device and on the server. Every slice that adds stored state
  buys its sentence in the same PR.
- **"Badge" is taken.** `src/content/badges.js` and `ui.jsx` mean earned,
  measured-never-awarded achievements. The unread chip is the **mail count** and its
  class is `mail-count`.
- **`mail:` is taken too.** `registry.js:510` stores email verify and reset tokens as
  `mail:<hash>`, swept from `p.mail` at `registry.js:820`. The post index moves to
  `letters:` rather than sharing that prefix. Same collision class as `rateGame`.
- **The shared checkout is shared.** Stage explicit paths. Build from a worktree.

## Premises

1. **The top-right cluster becomes `[Lang] [Mail ·3] [You]`.** The Look entry moves
   to the Profile screen. `src/views/Look.jsx` **stays a top-level route** — only its
   top-bar entry point moves, and it goes near the top of Profile, not buried: its
   discoverability was fixed deliberately once before. `topbar.look` and
   `topbar.lookShort` are retired in all nine `src/i18n/<lang>/shell.js`; `topbar.mail`
   is added there and the Profile entry under the existing `profile:` block in
   `src/i18n/<lang>/screens.js`.
2. **An unread count is not a read receipt.** The count tells *you* what you have not
   read. It never tells the sender you read them, and the cursor is never readable by
   the other party.
3. **The roll may order by relationship; it may never measure attention.**
   `legal.js` bans analytics scripts, ad networks, tracking pixels and counting
   visits, and draws the line itself: *"A game is not a visit and an account is not a
   visit."* Arithmetic over deliberate public acts is allowed. **Allowed:** order by
   people you have played, friended, or written to, then recency. **Forbidden:** view
   counts, "seen by", impressions, trending, likes as a sort key.
4. **Chat during a game never changes route.** The dock mounts above the router. The
   board stays mounted, the clock keeps running, the socket is not torn down.
5. ***Writing* reach does not widen.** You may still only write to a friend or somebody
   you have finished a game against; blocking stays one-sided and silent. Records that
   are already public may be *shown* read-only without that being a widening.
6. **Build happens from a fresh worktree off `main`.** The checkout this was designed
   in is 679 commits behind.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Two independent reviewers with no sight of the conversation read the design.

**The first** contributed the diagram atom and "the reply is a move," now the spine of
the approach: *"Ship that atom once and the mail, the sidebar and the feed all become
the same feature."* It read "almost" and "the feed must be something users must see" as
descriptions of *presence*, not publishing. It warned that *"a mail icon with a permanent
zero is a worse outcome than no mail icon,"* which is why discoverability ships in the
same slice as the count. It challenged premise 3 on wrong evidence (`featured.js` is a
manual pin, not a ranking) but was right anyway on better evidence found while checking,
and premise 3 was revised.

**The second** (the engineering review's outside voice) found what the earlier passes
missed, and reversed two decisions the review had already made. Its findings are recorded
in the review report at the end of this document. The three that changed the architecture:

- A K-way time-merge does not implement "order by relationship, then recency" — it
  implements *filter* by relationship then pure recency, which is the failure mode the
  first reviewer named, restricted to 25 people instead of the whole club.
- `mail:` is an already-occupied namespace, and the mirrored index write would have
  clobbered the read cursor on every incoming letter.
- `seq` cannot live on a letter: `readThread` maps each one to exactly `{from, text, at}`
  and `withLetter` does `.slice(-100)`, so array position is not monotonic. `at` already
  is, and already survives the round trip.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The Post, found
Read cursor and unread count, put "write to them" on player cards and the end-of-game
screen. Stop.
S (human ~2 days / CC ~45 min) · Low risk · Completeness 4/10.
**Rejected:** answers one third of the brief. Survives intact as Slice 2.

### Approach B: The atom first — **chosen**
The whole-board atom as the shared unit of content, then the mail count, then the reply,
then the roll, then the dock.
XL (human ~3 weeks / CC ~4-6 sessions) · Med risk · Completeness 9/10.

### Approach C: The dock and the roll
One collapsible right panel above the router, two tabs, text-first, positions later.
L (human ~2 weeks / CC ~3 sessions) · Med-High risk · Completeness 7/10.
**Rejected on one ground:** a text-only roll is a permanent precedent. Once rows are
prose, positions never arrive. Its structural idea — mounting the panel above the router
so premise 4 is true by construction — is adopted into Slice 5.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B, in five slices.** The reply moves to third: it needs only the atom and a
letter that can carry one, and it is the reason this approach was chosen, so it does not
go last where things get cut.

### Slice 1 — the atom

New: `src/engine/diagram.js` (pure, no React).

```js
{
  size,                       // 9 | 13 | 19
  cells,                      // flat array, "b" | "w" | null — the engine's own shape
  toPlay,                     // "b" | "w"
  bounds                      // { c0, r0, c1, r1 }, INCLUSIVE, display only
}
```

**The atom carries the whole board. `bounds` is a crop to look through and never
affects legality.** Go rules are not local: a chain that leaves the crop has liberties
the crop cannot see, so a fragment is not a position and cannot be played on.

- `boundsOf(cells, size, pad)` — the crop, computed from where the stones are. `pad`
  is in board lines.
- `diagramAt(record, moveNo, pad)` — an atom from a game record at a move. Lands in
  Slice 3 with its first consumer.
- `playOn(atom, move)` — delegates to the existing rules kernel on the **full** board
  and returns the next atom, or `{ ok: false, reason }` in the convention `rules.js`
  already uses. Never consults `bounds`. Lands in Slice 3.

Serialisation reuses `sgf.js` for the board and `PL` for `toPlay`. **SGF has no crop**,
so `bounds` is re-derived with `boundsOf` on read; the round-trip test asserts board and
`toPlay` exactly, and crop by re-derivation.

New: `src/components/Diagram.jsx` — renders an atom through the existing `Board` at card
size, windowed by `bounds`. Marks paint under stones, so an overlay on an occupied point
needs the `pointed` prop. `StoneFace` already scales; the white rim stays an attribute.

**Scope:** the atom type, `boundsOf` and `Diagram.jsx`. These are the shared primitive
and are useless separately; their first consumer is Slice 3.

### Slice 2 — the mail count, and the Post found

**The index moves to `letters:<me>:<other>`** — `mail:` is taken by verify and reset
tokens (`registry.js:510`, swept at `:820`), and a prefix list over `mail:` would walk
both record types.

```
letters:<me>:<other> = {
  at,         // last letter in the thread, for byRecent — as today
  theirLast,  // the `at` of the last letter FROM them
  read        // the `at` this side has read up to
}

unread(row) = row.theirLast > row.read
```

**No `seq`.** `at` is already monotonic per thread and already survives `readThread`,
which maps each letter to exactly `{from, text, at}`. Introducing a sequence number
would mean changing the thread's stored shape from an array to an object — a second
migration, on the one value in the system that is user correspondence.

**The two rows now hold different values**, which is what makes the mirrored write safe.
`registry.js:1177-1181` currently puts one object to both keys; under this shape it must
build each side separately, or the sender's cursor lands in the recipient's row and
zeroes their count on every incoming letter.

**The count is of threads, not letters.** Three people wrote to you reads as 3.

Server, pure first, in `server/post.js`:
- `unreadRows(rows, blocked)` — the top-bar total and the per-row dots, with the blocked
  filter applied once, here.
- `unreadIn(thread, read)` — for the open thread, where the letters are loaded anyway.
- **Rewrite the POST header comment.** It states the post has "no typing indicator, no
  read receipt and no notification." After this slice two of those three are still true.
  This repo treats header comments as checkable claims.

Also in Slice 2, because Slice 2 is what makes it hot:
- **`met:<a>:<b>` and `metDone:<a>`.** `#havePlayed` (`registry.js:1132`) lists an
  archive prefix with no limit. Slice 2 puts `mayWrite` on every player card, which is
  overwhelmingly strangers — every one a miss. `met:` is existence-only, written in
  `noteGame`'s finished branch; `metDone:` marks a player whose archive has been walked
  once, so a miss becomes authoritative and the unbounded scan runs at most once per
  player, ever. Mirror rows are swept on account delete the way `#forgetPost` sweeps
  `letters:<other>:<id>`, and in the fold-handles path (`registry.js:710-786`).
- **This reverses a documented refusal.** `registry.js:1128` says a list of everybody
  you have played "is exactly the data this feature exists to avoid needing." That
  comment is rewritten, and the reversal is recorded in `DECISIONS.md` with its reason.

Client:
- `src/net/api.js` — `markRead` beside `letters` / `thread` / `write`.
- `src/App.jsx` — cluster becomes `[Lang] [Mail ·n] [You]`; the Look entry moves to
  Profile, route unchanged.
- **One total in the top bar; the trainer card keeps its local indicator.** The
  trainer's unread (`unread(box)` in `src/store/sensei.js`) and account letters sum into
  the single number on the mail icon. The `.letter-unread` chip on `Home.jsx` is
  restyled to share `.mail-count`, so there is one chip in the stylesheet.
- **Discoverability, same slice:** "write to them" on `playerCard.js` and the end-of-game
  result path, gated on `mayWrite` and hidden silently when it refuses — a button that
  explains why you may not write tells you they blocked you.
- `src/content/legal.js` — sentences for the read cursor and for `met:`.

### Slice 3 — the reply is a move

This is the slice the approach was chosen for. It needs only Slices 1 and 2.

- `playOn(atom, move)` and `diagramAt(record, moveNo, pad)` land here.
- A letter may carry an atom and a question. The recipient answers by **playing on the
  board in the letter**. Their move becomes the next letter in the thread, with the
  resulting atom attached.
- The Worker validates the replied move with the same `playOn`, because the engine is
  pure and this is what that invariant was for. An illegal reply is refused server-side
  with the same `{ ok: false, reason }` the board uses.
- Once a reply is a move, the cheap wins arrive nearly free: `relations.js` can name the
  shape the answer made, and the shipped human net can say what it would have played.
  Neither is required for the slice to be worth shipping.
- Reach is unchanged — a position lands in an existing thread, and `mayWrite` gates it.
- **Open before building:** whether a replied move notifies, and whether a thread of
  replies is a line or a tree. A line is much simpler and is the default.

### Slice 4 — the roll

**One capped global index, not a per-viewer merge.**

```
roll:<14-digit stamp>:<gameId>      cap ROLL_KEEP = 400
```

One write in `noteGame`'s finished branch, which already writes two to four archive keys
there. One read: `list({ prefix: "roll:", reverse: true, limit })`, the idiom
`archivePage` already uses, with a cursor that already works.

**Relationship is a filter applied in memory over those 400 rows**, ordered
`met:` → `friends:` → `letters:` → recency. This is what makes premise 3's ordering
actually implementable: a time-merge over K streams gives filter-then-recency, which is
the "most active member drowns everyone" failure wearing a smaller hat.

- `server/roll.js` (new, pure) owns the ordering and filtering over rows handed to it by
  Registry. `archive.js` stays about one player's keys, as its header says.
- Each row carries an atom (`diagramAt`) and a "walk it" affordance into review.
- **A blocked person's rows never appear**, filtered after the read, before the page.
- **What emits a row:** finished games between accounts. **Not** cleared lessons or rank
  moves — progress is account-private. **Not** kept positions: `src/store/graphs.js` is
  local-only and the screen that draws the graph promises *"it runs on your own machine:
  nothing about this game is sent anywhere."*
- **Cache:** one global cached page with the `ladderCache` shape (`registry.js:1867`) —
  a single object with a TTL, dropped to null in `noteGame`'s finished branch. Not N
  per-viewer caches: knowing which viewers a game invalidates is the reverse follower
  map, which is the fan-out this design rejected.
- **Privacy, same PR:** a `legal.js` clause naming the roll, the `ROLL_KEEP` cap, and
  deletion with the account.
- Degrades honestly: with no relationships yet, show featured games and house players —
  read-only public records, per premise 5.

### Slice 5 — the dock

- A collapsible panel mounted in the `App` shell **above** the router, so premise 4 is
  structural. Two tabs: the roll and talk.
- Talk reuses `hallSocket` for halls; pair threads stay request/response.
- Mirrors in RTL. Collapses to a rail below 760px, where the board owns the width.
- **Proof, not hope:** a mount counter asserted in a component test (opening the dock
  does not increment the board's mount count), plus a CDP pass on a live online game
  watching the clock tick across an open and close.

## Success Criteria

- A player who signs in after a day away can tell, without clicking, that other people
  were here and what they did.
- A player finishing a game can write to their opponent without visiting Profile.
- **People start writing to each other.** Asked, not measured: the Guatemala group is a
  handful of people you know, so the honest instrument is asking them. There is
  deliberately no metric behind this.
- No screen in the app displays a count of how many people looked at anything.
- The board never remounts and no clock stutters when the dock opens mid-game — proved
  by the mount-counter test and the CDP pass.
- **The top-bar total and the per-thread state agree** after a `THREAD_KEEP` trim, after
  `#forgetPost`, and after a block.
- `npm run build`, `npm test` and the nine-language parity suite pass on every slice.

## Amendment, 23 September 2026 — constraints 2 and 3 reversed by the owner

This section is appended rather than edited in. The review above is what two reviewers
concluded on 17 September and it should stay readable as that; what follows is the owner
overruling two of its constraints afterwards, on purpose and with the reasoning recorded.

**What was reversed.** Constraint 2 ("an unread count is not a read receipt", and the
cursor is never readable by the other party) and constraint 3's forbidden list, which
named *view counts* and *"seen by"* explicitly. Both were asked for directly: read
receipts you can turn on and off, notifications, and view counts.

**What was built instead of a flat reversal.** The constraints were about *measuring
somebody's attention without their say*, and that part is kept:

- **Receipts are the reader's switch**, off until turned on, and never the writer's. The
  cursor is copied onto the writer's row only while it is on; turning it off writes 0
  back, so a thread whose reader never turned it on and one whose reader turned it off
  are indistinguishable. Nothing is computed from attention at read time.
- **Notices carry no payload.** The push is an empty, signed POST; the service learns
  that something happened and not who wrote or what. The words shown come from the
  browser's own cache, not from the wire, which is why `server/push.js` has no RFC 8291
  encryption in it: the cheapest way to keep a letter from the push service is not to
  send it.
- **The view count is one integer per game** — not who, not when, not for how long, not
  from where — it is **never a sort key**, so a game cannot climb the roll by being
  looked at, and it falls off with its row. Counting requires a handle, which buys no
  data (the token is checked and dropped) but keeps the counter out of reach of a script.

**What this cost.** `legal.js` previously promised *"there is no view count here and
there is nothing to become one."* That promise is now withdrawn, and the notice says the
new thing in a sentence of its own, as it undertook to do. A published privacy promise
being withdrawn is the real price of this change, and it is recorded here so that nobody
later reads the reversal as an oversight.

**Still open.** Open Question 4 (dock versus club Hall) remains open and ungated by this.
