# Design: The social layer (a page, the people on it, and the post)

## Problem Statement

Joseki can seat two strangers at a board and rate the game honestly. It cannot do the
thing a club does: let one player look at another and recognise them. There is no page
for a person. There is no way to say "this one is my friend". There is no way to see
that a friend is here now, no way to keep a game worth keeping, no way to write to
somebody between games, and no one place that answers "whose move is it, in all of my
games, right now".

The ask is seven features. They are not seven features. They are one page, the people
standing on it, and a post between them.

## Demand Evidence

First-party and specific, from the founding conversation recorded in
`docs/designs/coaching-shape-commentary.md`:

> "It started because I belong to the Go Guatemala association. I wanted to create my own
> server to play my friends because the options online are ugly, feel not safe or hard to
> use. Chat and having my friends will be very important and they can be the first users
> soon."

A named group of real people, already playing go together, already dissatisfied. "Having
my friends" is the ask in this document, said out loud, before any of it was built. The
word **safe** in that sentence is the constraint that shapes every default below.

## Status Quo

What a player can do today, and where it stops.

| The ask | On `main` today |
| --- | --- |
| Say something about yourself | **Shipped.** One paragraph (280) and three facts — where you play, playing since, what you like to play — in `server/profile.js`, edited from `Profile.jsx`. |
| A picture | **Shipped.** Squared and squeezed to 192 px in the browser (`src/net/avatar.js`), stored under `avatar:<id>`, served immutable. |
| Somewhere to show it | **The route is live and nothing links to it.** `GET /api/players/:id`, `server/index.js:226`. Written down as an open item in `TODO.md`. |
| Keep the games you played | **Half.** `games:<id>` holds a capped list of summaries (`registry.js:535`); the full record lives in its Room Durable Object and is never deleted. Bot games never reach the Worker at all. |
| Friends | Nothing. |
| Know who is online | `lastSeen` is on every player record. The lobby socket knows who is connected. Neither is shown. |
| Feature your best games | Nothing. |
| Badges | Nothing. |
| Write to somebody | In-game chat only (`server/room.js`, "Table talk"). Nothing between games. |
| One board of all your games | `GET /api/games` returns them. There is no screen. **Clocks online do not exist** — an open Phase 4 item. |

So two of the seven are already built and unlinked, two are half-built, and the
timing half of the dashboard is blocked on work that is not in this phase.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

A member of Go Guatemala who already knows the person across the board. The narrowest
version worth shipping is **PR 1 alone**: a page with your face, your paragraph, your rank
and your record on it, reachable from every row of the ladder. That is the smallest thing
that makes a club feel like a club, and it costs one route that is already written and
tested.

## Constraints

- **10 ms of CPU per Durable Object invocation** on the Workers free plan. The Registry
  already scans `list({ prefix: "player:" })` for the ladder. Nothing here may add a
  second full scan to a hot path.
- **`src/content/legal.js` is a shipped promise**, linked from the foot of every page,
  governed by "no sentence describes behaviour the code does not have". Line 244 requires
  any new collection to be disclosed in a sentence of its own.
- **The engine is pure and stays pure.** Nothing in `src/engine/` learns about friendship.
- **The two-shadow neumorphism and the 12 px type floor** apply to every new screen.
- **`DELETE /api/me` must keep meaning what it says.** Leaving has to take the
  friendships, the mail and the featured games with it.

## Premises

1. **The public player page is the keystone, and its route already exists.** Friends,
   presence, featured games and badges are all things you look at on a page that has not
   been built. It ships first and everything else hangs off it.
2. **Every feature here is a new collection of personal data.** A friends list, a presence
   flag and a mailbox each need their sentence in `legal.js` in the same PR that adds
   them, and their line in `remove()`. This is a per-PR checklist item, never a tidy-up
   phase at the end.
3. **Presence defaults to off-the-record.** The founding complaint about other servers was
   that they "feel not safe". Friends see that you are here; the world sees that you
   played recently. Broadcasting to strangers is opt-in, and the opt-out the ask called
   for is therefore the default.
4. **"Active games with timing" is blocked on online clocks**, which is an open Phase 4
   item and not in this phase. The dashboard ships with whose move it is and how long the
   board has been waiting, and gains a clock when the room gets its alarm. The two do not
   get coupled, and the screen says which it is showing.
5. **Badges are measured, never awarded.** A badge is a fact the server can recompute from
   the record it already holds. "Fifty games" is a badge. "Good sportsmanship" is a claim
   and does not ship. The same rule that governs the eval numbers in
   `docs/designs/masters-and-books.md`.
6. **Mail is not chat and it is not a list.** Two people who have played each other, or
   who are friends, may write to each other. No broadcast, no mailing list, no unsubscribe
   link because there is nothing to leave, rate-limited, and blockable from the first
   commit. Anything wider is a spam surface on a free Worker with the founder's name on it.
7. **A friend is a person, and a club is a group of them.** This phase builds the person.
   The club is Phase 10 and sits on the same edge.

## Approaches Considered

**A: the page, then the people.** Eight sequential PRs, each one shippable and visible on
its own, growing outward from the player page. Chosen.

**B: social spine first.** One large pure `server/social.js` landing friendship, presence,
mail and badges with full tests, then two PRs of UI. Ruled out: nothing is usable until
the third PR, and a schema mistake in PR 1 is already inside six features by the time
anybody clicks on it.

**C: club-shaped.** Reframe the whole phase around clubs — Go Guatemala is a club you
join, members see each other, the club has a ladder and a board. Genuinely distinctive and
matched to the real first users, but "add this one person I met at a tournament" has no
home in it. Deferred to Phase 10, on top of the friend edge built here.

## Recommended Approach

**A, in eight PRs.** Each carries its own server change, its UI, its tests, and where it
collects anything new, its sentence in `legal.js` and its line in `remove()`.

1. **The page.** A view on the live `GET /api/players/:id`, linked from every row of the
   global ladder. Face, paragraph, three facts, rank with its question mark when RD is
   high, and the record. **No games list**: `legal.js` limits a finished game to the
   players and anyone holding the link to its room, and a list on a page anybody can open
   is wider than that sentence, so the games wait for PR 4 and travel with the sentence
   that permits them. **No table-seat links either**: `linkedGame()` in `Play.jsx` spends
   the `?game=` in the address on first read, so navigating away from a live online table
   strands the player in the lobby. Those links wait for PR 6, which is the way back.
2. **Friends.** Request, accept, decline, remove. Stored per player as a small list on
   each side of the edge, so reading your friends is one key, not a scan.
3. **Presence.** The Registry already holds the lobby sockets. `online` for friends by
   default; `showOnline: "friends" | "everyone" | "nobody"` on the profile; strangers get
   "played recently" from `lastSeen` and nothing finer.
4. **The archive.** Lift the `KEEP_GAMES` cap for finished games, serve one page of them,
   and give every archived game its SGF from the record the Room already keeps.
5. **Featured games.** Pin up to three onto your page, with one line of your own about
   each. The pin is an id; the game is still the record.
6. **The dashboard.** Every game you are in, ordered by who is waiting on whom, with the
   board's idle time. Says plainly that clocks are not here yet.
7. **Badges.** Computed at settle time from the record, never granted. A small, closed set
   named in one pure module and tested against it.
8. **Mail.** One thread per pair, rate-limited, blockable, deleted when either leaves.

## Open Questions

- Whether a friend request needs a note attached to it, or whether a bare request from a
  named player is enough. Shipping bare; a note is one field if it turns out to be missed.
- Whether the archive cap can be lifted without a storage bill. Finished-game summaries are
  small, but the number is unbounded per player, so PR 4 paginates from the first commit
  rather than discovering the ceiling later.
- Badge set is deliberately not enumerated here. It gets settled in PR 7 against the fields
  the record actually holds, so no badge is designed for data that does not exist.

## Success Criteria

- A Go Guatemala member can find another member's page without being told a URL.
- A friend request round-trips in under a minute of wall time, on a phone.
- `legal.js` still passes `legal.test.js` after all eight PRs, with a sentence per
  collection and no sentence describing behaviour the code does not have.
- `DELETE /api/me` leaves nothing behind: proven by a tool in `tools/server/`, the way
  `accounts.mjs` and `profile.mjs` prove their slices.
- No route added here adds a full `list({ prefix: "player:" })` scan.

## Dependencies

- **Clocks online** (open, Phase 4) gates the timing half of PR 6 only.
- **Letters** (`feat/mail`, PR #56, unmerged) is blocked on a domain and is unrelated to
  PR 8: that is post between players and never leaves the Worker.
- Every PR deploys the Worker through `deploy-server.yml` on merge to `main`.

## The Assignment

Ask one Go Guatemala member — a specific person, by name — to make an account on
sente-server this week and tell you the first thing they went looking for and could not
find. PR 1 is a guess about what a player wants to see about another player. One real
answer is worth more than the other seven PRs of speculation.
