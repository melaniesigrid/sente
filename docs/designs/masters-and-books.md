# CEO Plan: Masters and Books

Generated by /plan-ceo-review on 2026-09-09 (revision 3, after two rounds of adversarial spec review)
Branch: plan/masters-and-books | Mode: SCOPE EXPANSION (greenfield; the ask was "get creative")
Repo: melaniesigrid/sente | Approach: C, style layer now, fine-tune as the upgrade (chosen by the user)

Two asks, one plan. (1) House players that play in the style of specific masters, learned
from their games. (2) A curriculum built on the books and classics of go, using their
analogies and philosophy, with the masters' games as lessons. They share one foundation:
a corpus of public-domain master games, measured, and one rule: the number on the card is
measured, never claimed.

## System audit

- Main is at v0.1.0.0. House players run KataGo's human-style network in the browser
  (`src/engine/kata/`). The network conditions on rank, opponent rank, era, time control,
  date and source. Sente's `encodeMeta` (`features.js:184`) hard-codes source KGS and two
  dates. KataGo itself ships a `proyear_<year>` profile (`SGFMetadata::makeHistoricalProProfile`):
  both ranks at inverse-rank 1, source GoGoD, date set to the year. That is "a strong player
  of 1846 as the GoGoD pros played", available today with a ten-line change to `encodeMeta`
  and one Python-generated fixture. It is the baseline every style claim is measured against.
- Training side, half-present: `tools/kata/export_human.py` exports the humanv0 checkpoint,
  which KataGo publishes for fine-tuning; a Python venv exists at `dev/kata-tools`.
- The lesson library (`docs/designs/lesson-library.md`): six tiers, seven tracks, a pure
  synchronous step reducer (`lessonStep.js`: states open, wrong, busy, review, solved; async
  only through `pending: { ms, action }` timers), a CI verifier over every position, and
  `library.test.js` requiring every lesson to carry a tier, a rank in band and 3 to 6 steps.
- Delight layer live: Moku speaks board facts, belts wear ranks, kata of the day, the daily
  duel with seeded network replies (single-threaded WASM, bit-identical logits per device).
- In flight elsewhere: `feat/board-sizes` (13x13 and 19x19 lobby). Masters need 19x19.
- Taste references: `content/lessons/tier1/two-eyes.js` (one idea per step, refutations),
  `engine/kata/bot.js` (one seam, honest null), `content/duel.js` (pure, date-keyed).
  Anti-pattern retired: the flat `LESSONS` array.

## Landscape

- Layer 1: commented game collections (Invincible: the games of Shusaku), proverb books,
  and guess-the-next-move, the oldest engagement loop in go teaching.
- Layer 2: BadukPop and Go Magic sell interactive courses and thousands of problems; the
  Korea Baduk Association's app teaches children in 24 chapters; KataGo v1.15 imitates ranks,
  eras and historical years, and ships its checkpoint for fine-tuning. Nobody ships "sit
  across from Shusaku" in a browser.
- Layer 3: a master's style is largely measurable from the record. Those measurements are
  worth building even if no model is ever trained, because they are the only way to know
  whether a bot plays like him. The eval comes before the model, and the eval is a product
  feature: the style-match number on the card.
- Rights: game records are facts, and the source says so: the collection at
  homepages.cwi.nl/~aeb/go/games states "I do not claim any rights on this collection. The
  games here are in the public domain." Commentary in modern books is copyrighted, so every
  word of analysis in Sente is original. The Classic of Weiqi in Thirteen Chapters (Zhang
  Ni, Song dynasty, c. 1050) and the go proverbs are public domain; modern translations are
  not, so Sente renders its own.

## Premise challenge

- "AIs that play like specific players" is a proxy. The outcome is that a learner meets a
  master's mind: sees his choices, feels the gap between their move and his, and can sit
  across from him. The direct path is his games, measured and taught, with a bot that plays
  his openings and leans his way. A fine-tune is the polish, not the foundation.
- Doing nothing: the bots stay anonymous rank profiles and the curriculum stays shapes.
  Fine, and forgettable. Class means a lineage.
- Rebuilt: nothing. New code is one engine module (`engine/style/`), a small extension of
  the sampler and the meta encoder, one build tool (`tools/masters/`), two step types.

## Part 1: masters, the style layer

### The baseline first: `proyear`

`encodeMeta` gains `{ pro: true, year }` and mirrors KataGo's `makeHistoricalProProfile`
whole: both ranks at inverse-rank 1; ratedness rated (slot 74 = 0); time control unknown
(slot 75 = 1, slot 79 = 0) with the default zero time fields feeding slots 82 to 85; source
slot GoGoD (the source block is `151 + source`, sources 0 to 6 = unknown, OGS, KGS, Fox,
Tygem, GoGoD, Go4Go; slots 158 to 191 are unused); date encoded from the year. The encoder
accepts years before 1800 (KataGo's clamp lives only in its CLI), which Dosaku will need. `tools/kata/gen_fixtures.py`
emits `proyear_1846` logits for the `open19` position and the JS port must match bit for bit,
as the existing fixtures do. This alone gives "a strong player of 1846". It is the control
arm of every eval below and ships in PR 1 as an honest bot on its own.

### The corpus (`tools/masters/`, build time, Node)

1. Fetch the public-domain collections into `tools/masters/raw/` (git-ignored) from a
   manifest of URLs and expected counts. v1 roster: Shusaku (470 games) and Jowa (293),
   two Edo masters who can be compared on the same era. The rest of the roster waits for
   the eval to prove the layer moves the number (see Scope).
2. Normalise through `engine/sgf.js` (input cap and named parse errors exist). Keep 19x19
   only. Tag every game with `handicap`, `komi`, `masterColor`, `year`, `result`. Drop
   games under 30 moves. Any record that does not replay is listed in the build log with
   file name and offset, and counted in `index.json`. Never silently.
3. Style and book are computed on games without setup stones only (`setup.b` and
   `setup.w` empty): handicap games skew the opening features, while sen-ai-sen games are
   even and stay in. Counts per bucket are recorded so the card can say "412 games without
   handicap". `CorpusTooThin` fires under 100 such games.
4. Measure with `engine/style/features.js` (pure, shared with the runtime). Definitions are
   part of the code, not prose, but the plan fixes them now:

   | Feature | Definition |
   |---|---|
   | opening corners | first own move in each empty corner, classed 3-3, 3-4, 4-4, 5-3, 5-4, other |
   | line of play | share of own moves on line 1, 2, 3, 4, 5+; phases by move number: opening 1 to 40, middle 41 to 150, endgame 151+ |
   | contact rate | share of own moves orthogonally adjacent to an enemy stone |
   | tenuki rate | share of own moves with Manhattan distance > 6 from the last enemy stone |
   | fighting | ataris given and captures made per 100 own moves |
   | sacrifice | own stones captured per game, and net stones traded |
   | thickness lean | mean Manhattan distance from a new own stone to the nearest own stone |
   | quadrant tempo | share of own moves in the quadrant (split at tengen; tengen and the two centre lines count as none) holding the most enemy stones |
   | header facts | game length, resignation rate, komi, year |

5. Book: `engine/style/symmetries.js` gives `transformPoint(t, c, r, N)`, its inverse, and
   `canonical(board) -> { hash, t }` (the minimum `hashBoard` over the eight transforms and
   the transform that produced it). Keys carry side to move (positional hashes ignore it)
   and only positions where the master is to move are stored, over the first 30 moves,
   entries with fewer than 3 games dropped. Expect 500 to 2,000 entries and under 30 KB per
   master, not tens of thousands: Edo fuseki is stereotyped in the lines Shusaku played and
   dies by move 8 elsewhere. Tie case: a symmetric position matches several transforms; any
   tying inverse yields a correct move, and the test says so.
6. Baseline measurement: games split 60/20/20 into train (book and style), dev (choose λ
   and the clamp) and test (the numbers on the card). `tools/kata/dump_logits.py` (existing
   venv) writes, for every dev and test position and a self-play sample, every logit within
   the sampler's floor of the maximum (at least the top 64) at the `proyear` profile of the
   game's year, to `tools/masters/fixtures/`; `eval.mjs` asserts the keep set is fully
   covered before applying the prior. The same style features over the network's own
   choices give the baseline vector.
7. Output `public/masters/<id>.json`: `{ id, name, years, games: { total, even, dropped },
   style: { master, baseline, spread }, book }` and `public/masters/index.json`.

### The runtime (`engine/style/`, pure, browser)

```
   masters/<id>.json ─► book lookup on canonical(position), master to move, ≥3 games
                               │ hit: sample the move from the book's counts (override;
                               │      the network is not asked; in a duel the draw uses
                               │      createRng(positionSeed(seed, canonicalHash)))
                               ▼ miss:
   GameRecord ─► encodeMeta({ pro: true, year }) ─► network ─► logits ─► softmax, floor
                                                                             │ keep set
                        stylePrior(style, rec)(i) for i in keep ─────────────┤ + λ · prior
                                                                             ▼
                                              choosePolicyMove(..., { bias, seed? })
```

- The book is an override, because "plays his openings" means his openings. The prior is a
  bias, applied to the kept candidates only (`choosePolicyMove` gains an optional
  `bias(i)`), so a master's lean reorders the network's candidates and never lifts a junk
  move that shares a surface feature above the floor.
- The prior is `(master − baseline) / spread` per axis, clamped, summed over the per-move
  axes a candidate exhibits: line, contact, tenuki, thickness, quadrant, atari given. The
  rest (sacrifice, game length, resignation rate, komi) are eval-only. Zero when master
  equals baseline. λ and the clamp are chosen on the dev split, never on test. Expect single-digit points of
  top-1 agreement from it; the eval decides whether it ships (below).
- Honest label, everywhere: "Shusaku (house bot): his openings from 412 games, then a
  strong player of 1846 with a small lean his way. Style match 61%." The number is read from
  `eval.json`, never typed.
- Board size: a master is 19x19 only. `profile.master` on any other size is a
  `StyleDataError` at the seam; the lobby row forces 19x19.
- Failure: missing or malformed masters JSON is a `StyleDataError` with file and reason.
  Rated games fall back to the `proyear` profile and the card says "playing as a strong
  player of 1846 without Shusaku's book today". A master duel awaits the book before its
  first reply; a missing book is the duel's existing "host unreachable" path, never a
  downgrade, so two devices with the same seed always see the same game.

### The eval (`tools/masters/eval.mjs`, CI, offline)

Over the test split and the cached `proyear` logits, report per master: top-1 agreement
with the master's move for (a) `proyear` alone, (b) `proyear` plus book, (c) `proyear`
plus book plus prior; and the style distance of each arm's choices from the corpus mean.
Dev and test numbers are both committed to `public/masters/eval.json`; a regression is a
diff. Ship rule for PR 2: the prior ships only if arm (c) beats arm (b) on both test numbers;
otherwise the book ships alone and the card says so. Same JS prior code as the browser; the
Python side only dumps logits, so there is one implementation and no new dependency.

### Phase 2, the fine-tune (after Phase 4's backend exists)

- KataGo's own training scripts from `b18c384nbt-humanv0.ckpt`, one adapter per master.
  Keep source GoGoD and the true game year in the meta row; the master one-hot goes in the
  unused tail (slots 167 to 172), so the base model's source signal is not overwritten and
  "plain proyear" and "master" inputs stay comparable. Tensor shape unchanged. Symmetry
  augmentation x8; regularise toward the base, because 470 games is about 50k master-to-move
  positions.
- Acceptance: the same `eval.json`. A fine-tune ships only if it beats arm (c).
- Serving decided by measured adapter size: browser or the Phase 4 server.

## Part 2: books, the curriculum

### Shelves, not tiers

Tiers stay the spine. A book lesson is a normal library lesson with an optional
`book: "proverbs"` key, so it lives in `LIBRARY`, carries a tier, a rank in band and 3 to 6
steps, and every existing helper (`isDone`, `nextLessonFor`, `searchLibrary`) works
unchanged. The shelf in Learn is a grouping over that key. Two new step types:

- `replay`: a game study. The lesson file embeds the game (`moves`, generated at authoring
  time from the corpus, plus `stops`); nothing at runtime reads the git-ignored corpus, and
  the CI verifier replays every move through `tryPlay` as it does for `sequence`. Between
  stops the board advances one move every 250 ms (`pending: { ms: 250, action: advance }`),
  and a "next stop" control dispatches `advance` until the stop. At each stop the learner
  places a stone. Scoring is data and synchronous: `answers` (the master's move, full
  credit) and `strong` (moves precomputed at authoring time as the `proyear` profile's top
  three after the floor at the game's year, partial credit: "a strong player's move, not
  his"); anything else
  shows `wrongTextFor(step)` or one of the stop's optional `refutations`. Reducer mapping:
  advancing = `busy` with `pending: { type: "advance" }`, guessing = `open`, scored =
  `solved` for the stop plus an `advance` action, refuted = `review`; `moveIdx` guards the
  stop index, so scoring twice at one stop is impossible. The lesson player writes
  `bookProgress` on every `solved` transition, so navigating away keeps the last completed
  stop. Replay lessons carry a tier and a rank like every lesson: the two v1 studies sit in
  Tier 5 at `1k` (Tier 6 is gated on Phase 4); proverbs sit in Tiers 2 to 4, each at the
  rank its idea first matters. The optional "at your level" panel
  asks the live network what a player of the learner's rank tends to play here; it renders
  only when `modelReady()` is already true, fills in after the score, and never starts the
  54 MB download from a lesson.
- `maxim`: an info step carrying a short public-domain line, its analogy in plain words, and
  one verified position that makes the line true. No exclamation marks; the library rule
  holds.

### The v1 shelf (in ship order)

1. **The Proverbs** (folk, public domain). Ten lessons, quiz plus refutation: hane at the
   head of two stones; the enemy's key point is your own; extend three from a two-stone
   wall; do not go fishing while your house is on fire (urgent before big); the one-two-three
   principle; play away from thickness; a ponnuki is worth thirty points; attach to weak
   stones, keep away from strong ones; the empty triangle; capture the cutting stone. The
   karate framing runs through this shelf: a proverb is a kata, a fixed form drilled until it
   can be broken on purpose.
2. **Games of the Masters**, two replays for v1, with the roster: Shusaku against Gennan
   Inseki, 1846 (the ear-reddening move) and Jowa against Akaboshi Intetsu, 1835 (the three
   brilliant moves). Records public domain, commentary original. Each ends with "sit across
   from him", a lobby link to the master at the lesson's rank.
3. **The Classic of Weiqi in Thirteen Chapters** (Zhang Ni, c. 1050), the following PR.
   Thirteen `maxim`-led lessons: the board and its stillness; counting advantage;
   beginnings; engagement; the empty and the full; knowing oneself; judging the position;
   reading intentions; the oblique and the straight; the subtle; names and numbers; the nine
   grades (which the belt system already echoes); miscellany. Original rendering of each
   maxim with a verified position. Its first line becomes Moku's home line: "The board is
   square and still; the stones are round and moving."
4. **The reading room**: modern books (Kageyama's fundamentals, Takemiya's cosmic style,
   Davies' tesuji, Attack and Defense) named as further reading with one sentence each and
   nothing quoted. Lessons on the same ideas are original. The lawyer agent checks the list
   before it ships.

### Engagement, honest and quiet

- Guess-the-move points per book, stored in the profile as `bookProgress: { [bookId]:
  { stops, score } }`, sanitised like `lessonsDone` (unknown ids count nothing), shown as a
  small meter on the shelf in the style of the kata attendance card. No streak in v1.
- Deferred until the shelf exists: Moku quoting the Classic on matching board facts, and a
  book-completion mark on the belt ribbon.

## Section 1: architecture

```
tools/kata/dump_logits.py (venv) ─► tools/masters/fixtures/*.json ─┐
tools/masters/{fetch,build,eval}.mjs ─► public/masters/*.json ──────┼─► engine/style (pure) ─► engine/kata/bot.js ─► views/Game
                                                                    │        ▲                       ▲
content/lessons/**/ (book key, replay + maxim) ─► content/library.js┘        │                       │
                                              └─► views/Learn ───────────────┴── "at your level" panel (display only)
```

Four paths for the style layer: happy (JSON present, book hit or prior on keep set); nil
(no JSON: `StyleDataError`, `proyear` profile, card says so; duel: host unreachable);
empty (book miss past move 30: prior only, by design, one line in the local telemetry ring);
error (fetch fails mid-session in a rated game: same as nil per query, the card flips back
when a later query succeeds; a duel never enters this path because it awaited the book).

State machine for a replay stop, in reducer terms:

```
 busy(advance) ──stop reached──► open ──answers──► solved ──advance──► busy
                                   │──strong────► solved(partial) ──advance──► busy
                                   └──other─────► review ──retry──► open
 Illegal: scoring twice at one stop (moveIdx); advancing past the last move (the step
 ends and the shelf records the score).
```

Coupling: `engine/style` depends on `engine/board`, `engine/zobrist`, `engine/rules`
(legal moves) and the masters JSON shape. `views/Learn` gains an optional dependency on
`engine/kata` for the display panel only; scoring never touches it. No view learns a rule.

Scale: static per-device assets; the only heavy call is the network, one query per bot
move, at most one per stop for the optional panel. Single points of failure: the masters
JSON and the model file, both static on Pages, both with named failures.

## Section 2: error and rescue map

| Where | Error | Trigger | Caught by | User sees | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tools/masters | `SgfParseError` (exists) | bad record | build | log line, dropped, counted in index | fixture |
| tools/masters | `CorpusTooThin` | fewer than 100 even games | build | build fails, master not emitted | yes |
| engine/style | `StyleDataError` | JSON missing, malformed, or master on a non-19x19 board | bot seam | rated: "without the book today"; duel: host unreachable | yes |
| engine/style | never `RangeError` at runtime | prior only scores `legalMoves` | n/a | none | yes |
| kata/bot.js | network null (exists) | model unavailable | Game | rated: heuristic; duel: host unreachable | exists |
| verifier | vitest assertion in `library.test.js` | stop past record end, illegal `answers` or `strong` | CI | never ships | exists |
| profile | unknown book id | renamed book | `sanitizeProfile` | counts nothing | yes |

No catch-all anywhere; the seam returns null or a named error and the view names the reason.

## Section 3: security and threat model

- Inputs are static JSON and SGF produced at build time from a public-domain source. The
  SGF parser caps input and never evals. Masters JSON is shape-checked on load (id, counts,
  numeric book entries) and rejected as `StyleDataError` otherwise.
- No user data leaves the device. `bookProgress` lives in the profile under the sanitiser.
- Names and likeness: the plan's rule is that a master must have died before 1950 to ship
  without a check. Shusaku (d. 1862) and Jowa (d. 1847) pass. Go Seigen (d. 2014), Takagawa
  (d. 1986) and any living player go through the lawyer agent's name-and-likeness check
  first. Dosaku (d. 1702) and Shusai (d. 1940) pass the rule and wait only on the eval.

## Section 4: data flow and interaction edge cases

- Replay: double-click at a stop (moveIdx guard); navigate away mid-lesson (the shelf keeps
  the last completed stop, not the partial); slow panel (score first, panel later); model
  not loaded (panel replaced by one sentence; score unaffected).
- Master bot: first move with no book hit (prior only); a master from a no-komi era (the
  card says his games are measured without komi and the bot plays with 7.5 today); the
  lobby forces 19x19 and hides the row when `index.json` is absent.
- Profile: `bookProgress` for a removed book counts nothing.

## Section 5: code quality

- One feature implementation (`engine/style/features.js`) used by build, eval and runtime.
  Python only dumps logits.
- Replay lessons are data (embedded moves, stops, answers, strong, refutations); the reducer
  does the rest.
- No new dependency. `symmetries.js` is small, pure and tested; `zobrist.js` is untouched.

## Section 6: tests

- `engine/style/features.test.js`: each feature on hand-built boards; corpus mean and
  spread on a three-game fixture; even-games-only filter.
- `engine/style/symmetries.test.js`: eight transforms round-trip; one canonical key for
  eight rotations of a position; the tie case yields a correct move under any tying inverse.
- `engine/style/prior.test.js`: zero when master equals baseline; bounded; a contact-heavy
  master pushes a contact move ahead of an equal-logit distant move within the keep set;
  never adds a candidate below the floor.
- `engine/kata/policy.test.js`: `bias` applies to the keep set only.
- `engine/kata/features.test.js`: `proyear_1846` fixture matches bit for bit.
- `engine/kata/bot.test.js`: a book hit returns the book move without asking the network;
  two seeded runs pick the same book move; `StyleDataError` yields the `proyear` profile;
  a duel with a missing book yields null.
- `content/library.test.js` (exists): replay moves replay; `answers` and `strong` are legal
  at each stop; maxim positions verified; book lessons carry tier and rank in band.
- `tools/masters/eval.mjs` in CI on the fixture corpus; `eval.json` committed.
- Browser: the duel check script gains a master variant (same moves, same replies).

## Section 7: performance

- Prior: one pass over the keep set, constant work each, well under 1 ms.
- Book: one canonical hash (eight `hashBoard` calls on a 19x19, about 3k xors) per position.
- Assets: under 30 KB per master, fetched on first challenge and cached; index under 2 KB.
- Replay: zero network calls for scoring; at most one per stop for the optional panel.

## Section 8: observability

- Build: per-master counts (fetched, parsed, dropped with reasons, even, book entries) in
  the log and in `index.json`; the card shows "412 even games".
- Runtime: the local telemetry ring buffer (Phase 3 item) records per game: master id, book
  hits, mean prior magnitude, style match against the corpus mean. Never leaves the device.
- Every fallback is a named line on the card and a `console.warn` with the file name.

## Section 9: deployment and rollout

- Static assets on Pages; nothing server-side until Phase 2 of Part 1.
- PR 1: `proyear` profile in `encodeMeta` with its fixture; corpus tool, features,
  symmetries, Shusaku and Jowa JSON, eval with arms (a) and (b); the Masters row with the
  book only. PR 2: the prior and `bias` in the sampler, shipped only if the eval says so.
  PR 3: `replay` and `maxim`, ten proverbs, two replays, `bookProgress`. PR 4: the Classic.
- Rollback is git revert; the Masters row hides itself when `index.json` is absent.
- Needs `feat/board-sizes` merged first.

## Section 10: long-term trajectory

- The style vector is the bridge to Phase 4 analysis and to the parking-lot item "play your
  past self": the same code on the player's own records.
- The fine-tune is measured against the same `eval.json`; the product does not change, only
  the number on the card.
- The shelf grows by files; a new book is content, not code.

## Section 11: design and UX

- Lobby: a "Masters" row under the house players, same card, one extra line and the
  measured number, seal letter for the portrait as with every bot, bot label unchanged.
- Learn: tiers as today; the shelf as a second heading with a quiet meter per book.
- Replay: the guess is a stone placed, the answer is the master's stone fading in, the
  panel sits where the counting card sits in scoring.
- Empty states: no index (row hidden), no progress (meter at zero, no copy), model not
  loaded (one sentence). A design review runs on the shelf and the row before they ship.

## Rights check: Ke Jie

The user asked for Ke Jie (b. 1997, living). The lawyer agent's name-and-likeness check ran
on 2026-09-09 (issue-spotting, not legal advice). Ruling applied to the plan:

- A bot named "Ke Jie" does not ship without a written licence from him or his agency. The
  exposure is BC Privacy Act s.3 and the Ontario appropriation tort in Canada, California
  Civil Code 3344 in the US, and PRC Civil Code articles 1012 to 1017 in China, and it
  sharpens the moment anything is sold. A bot that loses at 5k under his name adds a
  false-light claim on top.
- "Style derived from Ke Jie's public games" is arguable in Canada and the US as truthful
  reference to a public figure, and not safe in China. Not the v1 route.
- An anonymised card is defensible everywhere: seal tint, "a top pro of 2017", the corpus
  count, and no name in the UI, the JSON `name` field, or surfaced SGF metadata. This is
  how a living player ships, if the eval justifies it. His games are not in the aeb
  collection; the source must state its terms, and `index.json` records source and terms
  per game. Fox and Tygem records are server logs under ToS and are not used.
- Any card carries: "House bot. Not affiliated with or endorsed by <name>. Opening book and
  style lean computed from N public game records; plays as KataGo's human-style network
  otherwise."
- The "died before 1950" rule holds for Shusaku, Jowa, Dosaku and Shusai. It does not clear
  Go Seigen (d. 2014) or Takagawa (d. 1986): California Civil Code 3344.1 runs 70 years after
  death and reaches non-Californian decedents. Both stay deferred behind the same lawyer
  question as Ke Jie.
- Game records themselves are facts in Canada and the US; the collection is the exposure
  under the EU database right. Other jurisdictions could not be confirmed.

Questions for a real lawyer before a living or post-1950 master goes to strangers: whether
the descriptive form survives PRC articles 1014, 1017 and 1020; whether 3344.1 is enforceable
against a Canadian publisher for Go Seigen and Takagawa; whether a bulk fetch from an
EU-hosted collection is a database extraction.
